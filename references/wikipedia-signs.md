Source: `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing` (retrieved and cataloged in full).

## 1. Content Patterns

### 1.1 Undue emphasis on significance, legacy, and broader trends
- **What it looks like:** Puffs up the importance of mundane or specific facts by claiming they represent a "pivotal moment," "broader movement," "enduring legacy," or "evolving landscape." Sometimes adds a hedging preamble ("Though it saw only limited application...") before inflating significance anyway. In biology/ecology, over-emphasizes generic ecosystem roles or conservation threats even when status is unknown.
- **Words to watch:** `stands/serves as`, `is a testament/reminder`, `a crucial/pivotal/vital/significant/key role/moment`, `underscores/highlights its importance/significance`, `reflects broader`, `symbolizing its ongoing/enduring/lasting`, `contributing to the`, `setting the stage for`, `marking/shaping the`, `represents/marks a shift`, `key turning point`, `evolving landscape`, `focal point`, `indelible mark`, `deeply rooted`, `distinction [matters/is important] because`.
- **Example:** *"The Statistical Institute of Catalonia was officially established in 1989, marking a pivotal moment in the evolution of regional statistics in Spain... This initiative was part of a broader movement across Spain to decentralize administrative functions."*

### 1.2 Claims that a topic has influenced discussions about related topics
- **What it looks like:** Situates a subject amid abstract "public discussions," "emerging policy discussions," or "philosophical questions," often paired with the rule of three.
- **Words to watch:** `has/have shaped conversations/discussions/debate about`, `generated debate about`, `prompted broader reflection`, `raising philosophical questions about`, `contributed to wider discussions`.
- **Example:** *"Collectively, these works have shaped emerging policy discussions about ownership, consent, and dignity in digital resurrection technologies."*

### 1.3 Undue emphasis on notability, attribution, and media coverage
- **What it looks like:** Tries to prove a subject matters by name-dropping outlet types (`trade publications`, `regional media outlets`) and listing where it was `featured in` or `documented across`, often echoing Wikipedia notability phrasing (`independent coverage`).
- **Words to watch:** `independent coverage`, `local/regional/national/[country] media outlets`, `music/business/tech outlets`, `trade publications`, `cited/featured/profiled in`, `extensively documented across`.
- **Example:** *"Her insights have also been featured in Wired, Refinery29, and other prominent media outlets."*

### 1.4 Undue emphasis on identification by primary or tertiary sources (2026+)
- **What it looks like:** Stilted, formulaic attribution of basic facts to official profiles, websites, or databases instead of stating the fact directly.
- **Words to watch:** `Databases/records/listings/filings/materials identify/list/describe [subject] as...`, `was identified by [source] as`, `is listed in`, `his/her professional profile lists`, `personal website identifies him as`.
- **Example:** *"His personal website identifies him as a retired Indian Administrative Service (IAS) officer associated with the 1982 batch..."*

### 1.5 Undue emphasis on online presence
- **What it looks like:** Notes that a person or organization "maintains an active social media presence" or "strong digital presence" with generic praise of their posts.
- **Words to watch:** `maintains a strong digital presence`, `active social media presence`, `actively shares the latest updates`.
- **Example:** *"The mall maintains a strong digital presence, particularly on Instagram, where it actively shares the latest updates and events."*

### 1.6 Superficial analyses (trailing `-ing` participle clauses)
- **What it looks like:** Tacks a present-participle (`-ing`) phrase onto the end of a factual sentence to inject shallow commentary on significance, impact, or symbolism.
- **Words to watch:** `highlighting...`, `underscoring...`, `emphasizing...`, `ensuring...`, `reflecting...`, `symbolizing...`, `contributing to...`, `cultivating/fostering...`, `encompassing...`, `enhancing...`, `valuable insights`, `align/resonate with`.
- **Example:** *"As of the April 2008 census, the population of Douera stood at approximately 56,998 inhabitants, creating a lively community within its borders."*

### 1.7 Vague expression of connection or association
- **What it looks like:** Avoids stating a direct role or relationship (e.g., "was CEO of") and instead says the subject is "connected with" or "associated with" it.
- **Words to watch:** `in connection with/to`, `connected with/to`, `in association with`, `associated with`.
- **Example:** *"He later became associated with musical education in Knokke-Heist, where he taught violin, viola and chamber music."*

### 1.8 Vague attributions and overgeneralization of opinions (weasel wording)
- **What it looks like:** Attributes claims to unnamed authorities (`Observers`, `Critics`, `Researchers`) or exaggerates a single source into a consensus (`Industry publications such as...` followed by an exhaustive list of two).
- **Words to watch:** `Industry reports`, `Observers have cited`, `Experts argue`, `Some critics argue`, `several sources/publications`, `described in scholarship`, `modern researchers treat`, `such as` (before exhaustive lists).
- **Example:** *"Due to its unique characteristics, the Haolai River is of interest to researchers and conservationists."*

### 1.9 Outline-like conclusions about challenges and future prospects
- **What it looks like:** Rigid ending sections or paragraphs titled "Challenges and Future Directions" / "Future Outlook" that open with "Despite its [positive trait], [subject] faces several challenges..." and close with vague optimism about ongoing initiatives.
- **Words to watch:** `Despite its... faces several challenges...`, `Despite these challenges`, `Challenges and Legacy`, `Future Outlook`, `Future Directions`, `continues to thrive`, `positions them as critical components`.
- **Example:** *"Despite its industrial and residential prosperity, Korattur faces challenges typical of urban areas... With its strategic location and ongoing initiatives, Korattur continues to thrive."*

### 1.10 "Awards and recognition" sections
- **What it looks like:** Formulaic `<X> and <Y>` section headers—especially `Awards and recognition` or `Recognition`—used to pad notability and legacy.
- **Example:** Adding a standalone `== Awards and recognition ==` section listing minor mentions or plaques.

## 2. Language and Grammar Patterns

### 2.1 High density of "AI vocabulary" words (by model era)
- **What it looks like:** Clusters of statistically overrepresented words that co-occur in post-2022 LLM prose:
  - **2023–mid-2024 (GPT-4):** `Additionally` (sentence-initial), `boasts`, `bolstered`, `crucial`, `delve`, `emphasizing`, `enduring`, `garner`, `intricate/intricacies`, `interplay`, `key`, `landscape` (abstract), `meticulously`, `pivotal`, `underscore`, `tapestry` (abstract), `testament`, `valuable`, `vibrant`.
  - **Mid-2024–mid-2025 (GPT-4o):** `align with`, `bolstered`, `crucial`, `emphasizing`, `enhance`, `enduring`, `fostering`, `highlighting`, `pivotal`, `showcasing`, `underscore`, `vibrant`.
  - **Mid-2025–mid-2026 (GPT-5):** `emphasizing`, `enhance`, `highlighting`, `showcasing` + heavy source/notability framing.
  - **Mid-2026+ (GPT-6):** `challenge`, `clearer`, `dependable`, `echoed`, `foster`, `leverage`, `matters`, `multifaceted`, `practical`, `prioritize`, `quietly`, `steady`, `universally` (paired with short 8–20 word sentences and period/comma-only punctuation).
  - **Grok-specific:** `causal`, `empirical`, `correlate`, `underscore`.
- **Example:** *"Somali cuisine is an intricate and diverse fusion... drawing from the rich tapestry of Arab, Indian, and Italian flavours... Additionally, Somali merchants played a pivotal role..."*

### 2.2 Avoidance of basic copulatives ("is"/"are"/"has")
- **What it looks like:** Replaces plain `is`, `are`, or `has` with puffed-up substitutes or marketing verbs.
- **Words to watch:** `serves as`, `stands as`, `marks`, `functions as`, `operates as`, `represents [a]`, `boasts`, `features`, `maintains`, `offers`, `holds the distinction of being`, `refers to`.
- **Example:** *"Gallery 825... serves as LAAA's exhibition space for contemporary art. The gallery features four separate spaces"* (instead of *"is LAAA's exhibition arm... There are four individual gallery spaces"*).

### 2.3 Negative parallelisms
Three distinct flavors used to sound punchy or corrective:
1. **`Not just X, but also Y` / `Not only X, but Y`:** *"The issue here isn't just sourcing—it's framing."*
2. **`Not X, but Y` / `It's not..., it's...` / `No..., no..., just...`:** *"Kusama's self-portrait is not a mirror but a portal: not a representation of self, but a mechanism for its constant reinvention."*
3. **`Y rather than X` (reversed, common in 2026+):** *"...prioritizing empirical consolidation of power amid fragmented loyalties rather than ideological purity."*

### 2.4 Treating list titles or broad topics as proper nouns in leads
- **What it looks like:** Defines a descriptive title or list as if it were a standalone proper noun using `refers to` or `is the chronological list`.
- **Example:** *"Historic churches of Lamont County refers to the historic churches, chapels, monasteries and other religious institutions located in Lamont County..."*

### 2.5 Rule of three
- **What it looks like:** Groups adjectives, nouns, or clauses into threes (`X, Y, and Z`) repeatedly to create artificial rhythm and false comprehensiveness.
- **Example:** *"The film preserves core elements such as Nezha's mythological image, magical weapons, and spirit of resistance while reshaping them through contemporary values, modern aesthetics, and digital animation technology."*

## 3. Style & Formatting Patterns

- **Title Case in headings:** Capitalizing every major word in section headers instead of sentence case.
- **Overuse of boldface (`**...**`):** Bolding key phrases mid-prose or misusing asterisks.
- **Inline-header vertical lists:** Bullet lists where every bullet begins with `**Bold Label**: Explanation`.
- **Incorrect heading levels:** Starting with `# Title` or using multiple `#` (H1) headings.
- **Emoji bullets/headers:** Prefixing headings or list items with emojis (`✨ About Me`, `🚀`).
- **Curly/smart quotes (`“ ” ’`):** Using typographic quotes/apostrophes where plain straight quotes (`" '`) are standard.

## 4. Communication Intended for the User (Leaked Assistant Artifacts)

### 4.1 Collaborative communication & chatbot preambles
- **What it looks like:** Leaving conversational filler, instructions, or HTML comments meant for the user inside the final text.
- **Words to watch:** `Certainly!`, `Of course!`, `I hope this helps`, `You're absolutely right!`, `Would you like...`, `let me know`, `here is a...`, `Delete this section before submission`, `<!-- SUBMISSION NOTES -->`.
- **Example:** *"Here's a template for your wiki user page. You can copy and paste this onto your user page and customize it further."*

### 4.2 Hedging disclaimers about source availability and recommended usage
- **2023–2024 (Didactic disclaimers):** *"However, it's important to note that..."*, *"It is crucial to differentiate..."*, *"It's important to remember..."*
- **2024–2025 (Source-gap speculation):** *"While specific details are limited/scarce..."*, *"not widely documented"*, *"in the provided/available search results"*, *"based on available information"*, *"maintains a low profile"*.
- **Mid-2026–present (Corrective framings):** *"[claim] should be treated as [symbolic/tradition] rather than [verified fact]"*, *"does not by itself establish..."*, *"should not be confused with..."*.

### 4.3 Phrasal templates and placeholder text
- **Mad-Libs blanks:** `[Your Name]`, `[Insert Date]`, `[brief reason]`, `(Add your channel URL here)`.
- **Date placeholders:** `2025-XX-XX`, `2022-11-XX`.
- **URL placeholders:** `INSERT_SOURCE_URL_30`, `PASTE_SPOTIFY_TRACK_URL_HERE`, `https://example.com/source1`.

## 5. Markup Irregularities & Citations

- **Markdown leakage in non-Markdown contexts:** Triple backticks ```` ``` ````, `---` dividers, `[text](url)`, `**bold**` in plain/wikitext fields.
- **Internal citation artifacts:** `contentReference[oaicite:1]`, footnote return arrows `↩`.
- **Broken external links & hallucinated DOIs/ISBNs:** Dead URLs not in Internet Archive, invalid ISBN checksums, or real DOIs pointing to completely unrelated papers.
- **Book citations without page numbers or URLs:** Valid-looking book entries with no page number or unverifiable page claims.
- **UTM tracking parameters:** `?utm_source=chatgpt.com`, `?utm_source=openai`, `?utm_source=copilot.com`, `referrer=grok.com`.
- **Unused named references:** References declared in `<references>` that are never cited inline.

## 6. Context-Specific Indicators (Comments, Edit Summaries, Miscellaneous)

- **Comment-specific tells:** Customer-service tone (*"If there are specific issues that need to be resolved, please point them out to me"*), fabricated policy shortcuts, long multi-heading replies, urging critics to *"focus on improving content rather than whether AI was used."*
- **Edit-summary tells:** Canned policy assurances (*"improved clarity, flow, and neutrality"*), mentioning what was *preserved/retained/avoided*, over-stressing *"sourced content"* / *"independent secondary sources"*, listing exact template parameters, or *"Addressing reviewer feedback."*
- **Miscellaneous:** Sudden shift in writing style vs. older human text; AfC "Submission statements" explaining why the draft meets guidelines; pre-placed `{{AfC submission|d}}` decline templates; canned user pages (`About Me`, `My Interests`, `Let's Connect!`); permissions gaming; model-specific idiolects (ChatGPT/Grok verbose & legacy-heavy vs. Gemini/Claude more concise); Western/high-resource training data biases.

## 7. Signs of Human Writing (What to Preserve/Emulate)

- **Simple `is`/`has` constructions:** `there is a`, `it has a`.
- **Plain verbs over stiff synonyms:** `wrote` (not `authored`), `moved` (not `relocated`), `used` (not `utilized`), `tried` (not `attempted`), `died` (not `passed away`).
- **Direct superlatives/definitives when true:** `one of the best`, `is the only`, `was the first`.
- **Natural qualifiers/intensifiers:** `very`, `perhaps`, `tends to`.
- **Ordinary connective phrasing:** `as a result of`, `in order to`, `all of the`, `a part of`, `the fact that`.
- **Ability to explain choices & typos:** Specific, concrete rationale rather than generic policy-speak.

---

## 8. Ineffective Indicators (Do Not Over-Weight Alone)

- Perfect grammar alone.
- Mixing casual and formal registers (common in technical writers).
- "Bland" or "formal/academic" prose without specific AI vocabulary/syntax tells.
- Single transition words (`Additionally,`, `Notably,`) in isolation.
- Missing citations or VisualEditor/copy-paste HTML quirks.

---

## 9. Historical Indicators (Older Models, Still Flagged)

- **Promotional/travel-guide puffery (2023–2025):** `Nestled`, `breathtaking`, `vibrant`, `rich cultural heritage`, `stands as`, `gateway to`, `tapestry`.
- **Knowledge cutoff disclaimers (2022–2023):** `As of my last knowledge update...`.
- **Section summaries (2023–2024):** Ending every section/essay with `In summary,`, `In conclusion,`, `Overall,`.
- **Prompt refusals:** `As an AI language model...`.
- **Em-dash overuse (2022–Sept 2026) & Em-dash over-correction (late 2026+):** Either excessive spaced em dashes (` — `) or *zero* alternate punctuation (only periods and commas with rigid 8–20 word sentences).
- **Abrupt cut-offs & outdated `access-date` parameters.**
- **Forced lexical diversity ("elegant variation"):** Refusing to repeat a normal noun (e.g., cycling through `these artists`, `non-conformist artists`, `like-minded artists`, `Russian avant-garde artists` in one paragraph).
